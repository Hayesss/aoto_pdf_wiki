Analysis of PRISM and repurposing datasets- Growth-inhibitory data for 1,448 compounds
screened against 489 cell lines at 8 doses from the PRISM Repurposing Secondary dataset (10)
was analyzed to identify drugs with similar profiles of growth-inhibition to elesclomol. Replicate
collapsed log fold change data was downloaded from depmap.org/repurposing (PRISM
Repurposing 19Q4, Secondary Screen). Pearson correlation coefficients between all the profiles
were calculated. For each pair of compounds, a similarity score was computed as the maximum
absolute correlation between any two doses of the compounds. The distance between each
compound pair was defined to be 1 - similarity score, and the pairwise distance matrix is embedded
into a two-dimensional space using t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding for the
visualization purposes. The embedding procedure is implemented using the Rtsne R package with
a perplexity of 10 and default settings. In the correlation analysis of PRISM data for elesclomol
previously described (5) with the repurposing data (10) or FDX1 gene expression, the pearson's
correlation (r) was computed for each profile, and p-values are computed using the corresponding
t-statistics. The p-values are corrected for multiple hypothesis testing using Benjamini-Hochberg
correction.

Immunoblot analysis- Cells were lysed in RIPA lysis buffer (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO)
supplemented with 1x HALT protease inhibitor (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA) and
allowed to homogenize on ice for 30 minutes after lysis. Cell debris was removed by centrifugation
for 10 minutes at 10,000 RPM and the debris pellet was discarded. Protein abundance was
quantified by the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) method using and bovine-specific albumin standard
curve for normalization of protein abundance. Aliquots of each protein extract were boiled in LDS
sample buffer, where specified 10µM TCEP (Thermo 77720) final concentration was added before
boiling. For the insoluble fraction, the cell pellet following lysis with RIPA was resolubilized in
RIPA with the supplementations of benzonase and then proceeded as normal samples. Size
fractionated by SDS-PAGE at by Bis-Tris 4-12% gels, and transferred onto nitrocellulose
membranes with pre-cast gels via the iBlot-2 system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA).
The membrane was then incubated at room temperature for 1-2 hours in LICOR Odyssey blocking
buffer and incubated at 4℃ with the appropriate antibody overnight. Following incubation, the
blot was washed 4 times with 1x TBS with 0.1% Tween20 and incubated with fluorophore-specific
IRDye secondary antibodies (LI-COR, Lincoln, NE) and imaged on a LI-COR Odyssey machine
where data was also quantified. Where specified statistical analysis was conducted by performing
multiple t-test with a desired FDR(Q) of 1% based on the two-stage set-up method of Benjamini,
Krieger and Yekutieli.

Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Segmentation - Ilastik, a random-forest based analysis tool (54), was
used to perform pixel classification on hdf5 files containing only the DAPI and Cy5 channels. A
subset of the collected images was annotated for training purposes. The Hoechst and Phalloidin
signals were used to label pixels as nuclear area and cytoplasmic area, respectively. Areas with no
signal were marked as background area. The resulting classifier was applied to the full dataset,
outputting probability masks for each area. These masks were inputted into a custom MATLAB
script to generate nuclear and cytoplasmic segmentation masks.
Foci Segmentation and Analysis - The DLAT signal was background subtracted (background
defined as the morphological opening of the image) and filtered using a Laplacian filter. The
filtered image was converted into a binary mask based on an empirically determined threshold.
The foci segmentation masks were used to extract foci count, area, and intensity measurements.
To account for potential nuclear segmentation errors, nuclei that did not meet certain
morphological and DAPI-based criteria, were filtered out. Foci below an area of 75 (empirically
determined value) corresponded to background signal and were filtered out. Total foci count was
normalized to total cell count in each well.

For analysis we initially filtered out guides with fewer than 25 reads in the plasmid DNA pool and
guides that targeted multiple genes. Then, the guide counts were normalized against a library size
factor estimated from the median of the negative control guides, similar to the normalization
performed in MAGeCK (56). The negative control guides were selected to be no site targeting
guides in the Brunello library. The guide-level log2 fold change was computed using the ratio
between the treatment versus the vehicle control normalized counts. The guide-level log2 fold
change values from all guides targeting each gene were median-collapsed to yield the gene-level
log2 fold change. Statistical significance for each gene-level result was calculated using the
MAGeCK-RRA method, with the no site guides as controls for generating the null distribution of
RRA and otherwise default parameters (version 0.5.7) (56). Two-sided p-values were corrected
for multiple hypothesis testing using the Benjamini–Hochberg method.
