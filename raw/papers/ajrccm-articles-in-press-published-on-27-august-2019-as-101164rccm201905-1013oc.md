Authors’ comments: This study identifies a new anti-inflammatory therapeutic strategy for
cystic fibrosis (CF). We show for the first time that alterations in CF neutrophil
immunometabolism drive pro-inflammatory cytokine release by these cells, an effect that
correlates closely with clinical outcomes and can be abrogated by a specific small molecule
inhibitor of the NLRP3 inflammasome. The data show that these immunometabolic changes
stem from chronic inflammation rather than an intrinsic CFTR defect and as such may occur
even in the presence of effective CFTR modulation.

Abstract
RATIONALE: Cystic fibrosis (CF) pulmonary disease is characterized by chronic infection with
Pseudomonas aeruginosa and sustained neutrophil-dominant inflammation. The lack of effective
anti-inflammatory therapies for people with CF (PWCF) represents a significant challenge.
OBJECTIVES: To identify altered immunometabolism in the CF neutrophil, and investigate the
feasibility of specific inhibition of the NLR family, pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3)
inflammasome as a CF anti-inflammatory strategy in vivo.
METHODS: Key markers of increased aerobic glycolysis, known as a Warburg effect, including
cytosolic pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2), phosphorylated PKM2, succinate, HIF-1α, lactate and the
interleukin (IL)-1β precursor pro-IL-1β, as well as caspase-1 activity and processing of pro-IL-
1β to IL-1β by the NLRP3 inflammasome, were measured in neutrophils from blood and airway
secretions from healthy controls (n=12), PWCF (n=16) and PWCF post-double-lung transplant
(n=6). The effects of specific inhibition of NLRP3 on airway inflammation and bacterial
clearance in a murine CF model were subsequently assessed in vivo.
MAIN RESULTS: CF neutrophils display increased aerobic glycolysis in the systemic
circulation. This effect is driven by low-level endotoxemia, unaffected by CFTR modulation, and
resolves post-transplant. The increased pro-IL-1β produced is processed to its mature active form
in the lipopolysaccharide-rich CF lung by the NLRP3 inflammasome via caspase-1. Specific
NLRP3 inhibition in vivo with MCC950 inhibited IL-1β in the lungs of CF mice (P<0.0001),
resulting in significantly reduced airway inflammation and improved Pseudomonas clearance
(P<0.0001).

Introduction
Interleukin (IL)-1β is a master inflammatory cytokine involved in the pathogenesis of insulin
resistance, atherosclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, gout and Alzheimer’s disease (1-5). IL-1β is also
strongly implicated in pulmonary disease, where it stimulates mucus hypersecretion and airway
hyperresponsiveness (6, 7), triggers further pro-inflammatory cytokine release (8), and impairs
clearance of key pathogens such as Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Aspergillus (9-11). The list of
immune cells capable of producing IL-1β includes monocytes, tissue macrophages, T-
lymphocytes, dendritic cells and neutrophils (12, 13). Human IL-1β is synthesized as the
precursor protein pro-IL-1β and requires cleavage to a mature, active form to exert its pro-
inflammatory effect, this cleavage typically occurring via the class I cysteine protease caspase-
1 (14, 15). Caspase-1 itself requires activation, initiated by inflammasomes in response to
activation of pattern recognition receptors such as toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) by environmental,
pathogenic or endogenous danger signals (16). The NLRP3 inflammasome is a composite of
adapter and effector proteins highly expressed in myeloid cells, containing NLRP3, adapter
protein apoptosis-related speck-like protein (ASC), and caspase-1 (16). Previous studies have
identified NLRP3 as a key sensor for phagocytosed material, insoluble protein aggregates and
metabolic products (1, 2, 4, 17-19) and the driver of IL-1β.
It has become increasingly clear that increased transcription of pro-IL-1β in response to infection
or tissue injury is fueled by changes in cellular metabolism. In healthy cells, aerobic metabolism
mostly occurs via an intact tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and oxidative phosphorylation to
generate adenosine triphosphate (ATP), while glycolysis is responsible for anaerobic
metabolism. However, in response to the TLR4 agonist lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and other pro-
inflammatory stimuli, some immune cells can shift to a state of increased aerobic glycolysis.

This metabolic shift, known as a Warburg effect, results in a rapid low-level yield of ATP and
production of lactate via the glycolytic pathway, with the TCA cycle reset to facilitate the
accumulation of the metabolic intermediate succinate. Succinate leakage into the cytosol acts to
stabilize the transcription factor hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) via inhibition of prolyl
hydroxylases. HIF-1α in turn translocates to the nucleus, where it interacts with the hypoxia
response element to transcribe pro-inflammatory cytokines - in particular IL-1β - and glycolytic
machinery (20, 21). The pyruvate kinase isoenzyme PKM2 has emerged as a pivotal determinant
of the Warburg effect in immune cells in response to LPS (22). PKM2 can assume different
conformations, which in turn exhibit differential enzymatic activity. Enzymatically inactive
cytosolic PKM2 dimers exist in equilibrium with tetramers. Dimers are capable of translocation
to the nucleus, where they directly interact with HIF-1α to regulate transcription (23).
While the roles of immunometabolism and NLRP3 in IL-1β production have been closely
examined in macrophages, monocytes and dendritic cells, the neutrophil remains
understudied. Since neutrophils are capable of generating IL-1β and outnumber other immune
cells in certain inflammatory conditions, a detailed knowledge of how IL-1β production by
neutrophils is regulated in human disease is of considerable interest. Cystic fibrosis (CF), a
complex autosomal recessive chloride channelopathy resulting from mutations in the cystic
fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) gene, is the most common lethal genetic
disorder affecting Caucasians and the archetypal model for chronic neutrophilic inflammation
(24-26). People with CF (PWCF) have significantly increased morbidity and decreased life
expectancy, primarily due to pulmonary disease, which is characterized by frequent
exacerbations and sustained inflammation, accentuated by airway mucus plugging and persistent
Gram-negative infection with P. aeruginosa (27-29). While the advent of CFTR modulators has

Here we investigate whether circulating CF neutrophils undergo metabolic reprogramming,
specifically a shift towards increased aerobic glycolysis, with a subsequent increase in NLRP3-
processed IL-1β in the lung. We elucidate the relative contributions of inflammation and CFTR
function to this process, and evaluate the effects of specific inhibition, both of Warburg
metabolism and the NLRP3 inflammasome, on LPS-induced IL-1β in CF lung disease. Some of
the results of these studies have been previously reported in the form of an abstract (37).

available in Supplemental Appendix 1. PWCF (n=46) were recruited from the Beaumont

fractions obtained using a nuclear extract kit, both performed at 4oC in the presence of protease
and phosphatase inhibitors. PKM2, phosphorylated PKM2 and HIF-1α were detected by SDS-
PAGE and western blotting. Pro-IL-1β, IL-1β and LPS were measured by ELISA. Cytosolic

CF-ABLE score
The CF-ABLE score is an easy-to-use predictive score for determining outcome in CF, validated
against the CF registry of Ireland (41). It accurately predicts the risk of death or transplantation
within 4 years. Scores range from 0-7, with a higher score predicting a worse outcome.
Statistical analysis
Results are expressed as mean+/-standard error of the mean (SEM) of biological replicates or
independent experiments. Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 for Windows. For
comparison of two paired groups a paired t-test was used. For comparison of two unpaired
groups, an unpaired t-test was used. Comparison of three or more unmatched groups was by
ANOVA, and quantification of the association between two variables was achieved by Pearson
correlation. A value of P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. Further details regarding
statistical analysis are available in Supplemental Table 6.

IL-1β levels are increased in CF airway secretions and correlate with markers of clinical
outcome.
IL-1β was increased in BAL from F508del/F508del PWCF (CF, n=12) compared to healthy
controls (HC, n=12; 316.2+/-30.87pg/mL vs 39.94+/-2.1pg/mL, P<0.0001, Fig. 1A). IL-1β levels
in BAL from F508del/F508del PWCF post-double lung transplant (Tx, n=6; 45.48+/-
8.434pg/mL) matched those from HC. BAL from patients with primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD),
a genetic condition that results in the development of severe bronchiectaisis (119+/-45.63
pg/mL) also displayed increased IL-1β levels compared to HC and Tx (P=0.01), with levels less
than for PWCF (P=0.009). CF BAL was neutrophil-predominant with neutrophils accounting for
71% of total cell count on average, consistent with previously published studies (42, 43). BAL
neutrophil percentage correlated strongly with IL-1β levels (R2=0.92, P<0.0001, Fig. 1B),
suggesting that the CF airway IL-1β burden was mainly neutrophil-derived. IL-1β in CF BAL
correlated with FEV (R2=0.83, P<0.0001, Fig. 1C). This correlation was maintained when

Confirmation of a Warburg effect in the CF neutrophil.
Other key markers of Warburg metabolism were similarly increased in CF neutrophils. Cytosolic
succinate levels were higher in circulating CF neutrophils (29.66+/-1.49nM, Fig. 3A) compared
to HC (7.523+/-0.27nM; P<0.0001) and Tx (7.924+/-0.37nM; P<0.0001). Spillover of succinate
into the cytosol acts to inhibit prolyl hydroxylases, thereby stabilising HIF-1α and facilitating its
translocation to the nucleus (45). HIF-1α was increased in circulating neutrophil cytosolic and
nuclear fractions from PWCF compared to HC (P=0.0002, P=0.0003 respectively, Fig. 3B, 3C).
Nuclear phosphorylated PKM2 was also increased in CF neutrophils (Fig. 3B). A further
hallmark of the Warburg effect is a shift in the metabolism of pyruvate away from the TCA cycle
entry molecule acetyl-CoA and towards lactate. Cytosolic lactate was higher in circulating CF
neutrophils (6.896+/-0.31nM) compared to HC (2.728+/-0.13nM, P<0.0001, Fig. 3D) and Tx
(2.636+/-0.21nM; P<0.0001). Cytosolic lactate:pyruvate (LP) ratio was similarly increased in CF
compared to HC and Tx neutrophils (both P<0.0001, Fig. 3E). Neutrophil cytosolic pH was

Discussion
In this study, we show for the first time that the CF neutrophil displays Warburg metabolism in
the systemic circulation. This shift relies on an intact PKM2/succinate/HIF-1α axis and results in
increased production of IL-1β, it’s processing at the lung driven by the NLRP3 inflammasome
via caspase-1. This inflammatory effect is abrogated both by inhibition of glycolysis and specific
inhibition of NLRP3 by small molecules in vivo, identifying the inflammasome as a therapeutic
target in CF.
PKM2 and phosphorylated PKM, previously identified as pivotal components of Warburg
metabolism, are increased in the cytosol of circulating CF neutrophils. This phenomenon is
amenable to inhibition by blocking glycolysis with 2DG. PKM2 expression is not influenced by
