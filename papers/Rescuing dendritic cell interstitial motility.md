## 方法概述

#### **Human patient SLN and tumour specimens**

Human SLN frozen sections were obtained from the Department of Pathology, Peking Union Medical College Hospital and the Hangzhou Cancer Hospital, Affiliated Hangzhou First People's Hospital. Patient information is provided in Supplementary Table 3. All specimens were selected as non-metastatic SLNs to avoid perturbation by invaded tumours. SLN categorization into early or late was based on a combination of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system, primary tumour stages and further examination of specific cases. For pancreatic cancer, SLNs from primary tumours at stages I–II were generally designated as early stages (*n* = 6) and from stages III–IV as late stages (*n* = 5). Moreover, stage II pancreatic tumours classified as T2 or higher were also considered as late stages. For breast and colorectal cancers, stages I–IIA were considered early stages (*n* = 5 for breast or 5 for colorectal) and IIB–IV as late stages (*n* = 6 for breast or 5 for colorectal). The breast cancer tissues for tumoural DCs were collected at the Affiliated Hangzhou First People's Hospital. All the study protocols were approved by the Institutional Review Board at Peking Union Medical College Hospital, Hangzhou Cancer Hospital and Westlake University.


## 讨论与结论

Here we identify sGC–PDE5-maintained cGMP as a key regulator of DC interstitial motility. As tumours progress, the TME becomes increasingly disordered, altering both biochemical and mechanical cues. These changes impair NO production and downregulate sGC, leading to diminished intracellular cGMP in DCs. Reduced cGMP levels weaken myosin-II activity, dampen DC interstitial motility and result in a progressive decrease in mig-cDCs in tdLNs. The loss of these antigen-bearing DCs impairs tumour-specific T cell priming and disrupts the cancer immunity cycle. Increasing cGMP levels through PDE5 inhibition restores DC motility, replenishes mig-cDCs in tdLNs and sustains antitumour T cell responses.

As a secondary messenger, cGMP is promptly generated by sGC after stimulation or quickly degraded by PDE533. Its turnover rate ranges from seconds to minutes. Compared with cAMP, the lower need of cGMP in the nanomolar range allows it to tune cellular processes more sensitively and flexibly38. Furthermore, a broad spectrum of factors, including NO, chemokines, cytokines, growth factors and mechanical forces, can activate sGC and trigger cGMP synthesis,33,,,49. The downstream effects of cGMP primarily involve engaging small GTPase to regulate myosin-II activity and adjust the cytoskeleton rearrangement. Given these properties, cGMP could function as a critical molecular switch that can converge multiple environmental cues inside DCs to steer downstream migration in a rapid, sensitive and tuneable manner.
