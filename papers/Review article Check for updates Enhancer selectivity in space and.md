## 摘要

The primary regulators of metazoan gene expression are enhancers, originally functionally defined as DNA sequences that can activate transcription at promoters in an orientation-independent and distance-independent manner. Despite being crucial for gene regulation in animals, what mechanisms underlie enhancer selectivity for promoters, and more fundamentally, how enhancers interact with promoters and activate transcription, remain poorly understood. In this Review, we first discuss current models of enhancer–promoter interactions in space and time and how enhancers affect transcription activation. Next, we discuss different mechanisms that mediate enhancer selectivity, including repression, biochemical compatibility and regulation of 3D genome structure. Through 3D polymer simulations, we illustrate how the ability of 3D genome folding mechanisms to mediate enhancer selectivity strongly varies for different enhancer-promoter interaction mechanisms. Finally, we discuss how recent technical advances may provide new insights into mechanisms of enhancer-promoter interactions and how technical biases in methods such as Hi-C and Micro-C and imaging techniques may affect their interpretation.



## 背景与目的

Precise regulation of gene expression in space and time is crucial for the development of a single-cell embryo into a complex organism[1](#page-13-10) . Hundreds of functionally distinct cell types arise during development, yet they share the same genome, suggesting that different genes undergo cell-type-specific activation[2](#page-13-11) . Enhancers are the primary component of metazoan gene regulation, thereby controlling spatiotemporal patterns of transcriptional gene activation[3](#page-13-12)[–6](#page-13-13) . Originally identified in 1981, enhancers were functionally defined as having the capacity to activate promoters regardless of their orientation and genomic distance from the transcription start sit[e7](#page-13-14)[–10.](#page-13-15) Despite recent efforts to define enhancers by their physical properties, such as occupancy of transcription factors or co-activators, presence of certain histone post-translational modifications (PTMs), depletion of nucleosomes or transcription of enhancer RNAs[11,](#page-13-16) unambiguous criteria are yet to be established.

Part of the challenge in defining enhancers is our lack of mechanistic understanding of how they interact with and activate promoters. Even though enhancers are capable of activating different promoters, transcription activation by enhancers is often restricted to a selected one or few promoter[s12](#page-13-17),[13](#page-13-18), often referred to as their cognate promoters. How do enhancers select cognate promoters over non-cognate ones? Here, we define enhancer selectivity as the fold increase in transcriptional output at cognate promoters over non-cognate promoters owing to the impact of a given enhancer.
