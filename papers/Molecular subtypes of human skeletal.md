## 方法概述

#### **Accrual of human skeletal muscle biopsies**

Rectus abdominis biopsy tissues were accessed from the Hepatopancreaticobiliary/Gastrointestinal Tumor Bank (University of Calgary, Alberta, Canada). Tumour bank participants provided written informed consent, and the tissue banking was approved by the Conjoint Health Research Ethics Board at the University of Calgary (ethics ID: E17213). Patients undergoing elective abdominal surgery were consecutively approached about participation in tumour and tissue banking. The study cohort and conditions for the acquisition of muscle samples were as described previously6,51. Briefly, rectus abdominis samples (0.5–3.0 g) were collected during open abdominal surgery scheduled as part of routine clinical care. An upper abdominal transverse incision was performed, and muscle biopsy was obtained at opening by sharp dissection, without use of electrocautery. Muscle biopsies were taken within 30 min of the start of the surgery and immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen to minimize ischaemic shock after devitalization. For histology, a subsample of the muscle was frozen in isopentane cooled in liquid nitrogen. All samples were stored at −80 °C until further use.

Rectus abdominis was chosen because it is accessible in many abdominal cancer surgeries and is the muscle most frequently chosen for study of cancer-associated changes2 . Whether rectus abdominis can be taken as representative of cachexia-related changes has not been proven; however, most of the putative influences on muscle in malignant disease (circulating tumour and inflammatory mediators, altered hormonal levels, deficiency of energy fuels and essential nutrients) have been suggested to be systemic9 .
