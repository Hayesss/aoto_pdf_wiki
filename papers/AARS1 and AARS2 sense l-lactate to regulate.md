## 方法概述

#### **Mice**

*Mct1fl/fl, Ldhafl/fl* and *Trex–/–* mice were from Cyagen Biosciences, *Aars2fl/fl* mice were from Gempharmatech, and *cGASKR/KR* and *cGASKQ/KQ* mice were from the Shanghai Biomodel Organism Center. All mouse models were generated through CRISPR–Cas9-mediated gene editing. *Lyz-cre<sup>+</sup>* mice were provided by the Shanghai Biomodel Organism Center. All mice were on the C57BL/6 background. Male and female mice were 8 weeks old, and all experiments used littermate controls. Mice were maintained under specific-pathogen-free conditions in the animal facility of Zhejiang University. The animal room has a controlled temperature (18–23 °C), humidity (40–60%) and a 12-h light–12-h dark cycle. Experimental and control animals were bred separately. Animal experiments involving infectious virus were performed in the physical containment level 2 laboratory of Soochow University. The Institutional Committee for Animal Welfare of the Soochow University approved and oversaw this study. All animal studies were conducted in accordance with guidelines of both Zhejiang and Soochow Universities.


## 讨论与结论

In this study, we showed that L-lactate antagonizes cGAMP production by increasing cGAS lactylation in cells. We identified AARS1/2 as intracellular L-lactate sensors and lactyltransferases that control the global lysine lactylome. AARS1/2 directly bind L-lactate and catalyse a chemical reaction for lactylation that only requires the participation of L-lactate and ATP in vitro. Aberrant uptake of L-lactate reduced innate immune surveillance through AARS2-mediated lactylation that inactivates cGAS.

Protein acviations represented by lysine acetylation, propionylation or succinylation are all catalysed by acyl-coenzyme A (CoA); therefore lysine lactylation was speculated to also require lactyl-CoA. However, the level of lactyl-CoA in mammalian cells and tissues are 20-350-fold lower than acetyl-CoA, propionyl-CoA and succinyl-CoA, which suggested that such lactyl-CoA-dependent mode of catalysis may not have a dominant role in regulating the lysine lactylome. In our study, nearly the entire lysine lactylome induced by L-lactate was erased by depleting both AARS1 and AARS2. Moreover, the lactyltransferase activity of AARSs is evolutionarily conserved from prokaryotes to mammals, which highlights the ancient role of AARSs besides participating in protein translation.

The traditional strategy for evaluating the role of, for example, a specific acetyl-lysine or lactyl-lysine modification is to express lysine-to-alanine or lysine-to-glutamine substitution mutants in cells. However, this approach cannot unambiguously determine and distinguish the effects of site-specific lactylation from other acylation. The genetic code expansion system developed here overcomes this limitation and enabled the conjugation of a lactyl moiety at specific targeted protein sites in E. coli and eukaryotic cells. In this case, enzymatic activity of cGAS was markedly prohibited by the addition of a lactyl moiety to its specific N-terminal residue.
