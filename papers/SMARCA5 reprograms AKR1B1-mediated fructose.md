## 背景与目的

Leukemia is an aggressive clonal hematologic malignancy characterized by aberrant self-renewal and immature hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells (HSPCs). Several studies have revealed genomic and epigenetic alterations of leukemia in regulating gene transcription, chromatin state, and DNA modifications, which are considered to be responsible for the rise of leukemia stem cells (LSCs) and leukemia initiation, progression, and relapse.[1–3](#page-16-0) Despite advances in conventional chemotherapy on acute myeloid leukemia (AML) over the past several decades, AML patients still have poor 5-year overall survival (average 5-year overall survival is less than 30%) due to relapse-related drug-resistant leukemia cells.[4–7](#page-16-1) Therefore, there is an urgent need to explore targeted therapeutic strategies.

## 主要发现

#### SMARCA5 is primarily responsible for aberrant chromatin accessibility in leukemia

To identify epigenetic factors responsible for aberrant chromatin accessibility of leukemia, we performed a CRISPR-Cas9 screening in murine AE9a leukemia cells by using a lentivirusbased epigenetic library that contains 214 epigenetic factors [\(Figure 1A](#page-2-0)). We found that small guide RNAs (sgRNAs) targeting *Smarca5* were among the most depleted ones in the screening, and *Carm1*, *Chaf1b*, and *Wdr5*, which were essential for the survival of leukemia cells in previous studies, were all significantly depleted [\(Figure 1B](#page-2-0))[.31–33](#page-17-6) Smarca5 is the core ATPase of the ISWI chromatin remodeling complex and is primarily responsible for the regulation of chromatin accessibility. Although SMARCA5 has a lower dependency in several healthy hematopoietic lineages (including HSC and granulocyte-monocyte progenitor [GMP]) than most of the other chromatin factors in single-cell *in vivo* CRISPR screening data [\(Figure S1A](#page-16-12)).[34](#page-17-7) Since leukemia cells exhibit dysregulation of chromatin accessibility, we hypothesize that Smarca5, as a major chromatin remodeler, may play a crucial role in leukemogenesis.

To uncover the function of *SMARCA5* in leukemia, we assessed the relative expression of *SMARCA5* from transcriptomic datasets of AML patients. The results showed mRNA expression of *SMARCA5* was elevated in leukemic cells and LSCs than healthy HSC in GEO: GSE74246[15](#page-16-6) and GEO: GSE63270[35](#page-17-8) datasets ([Figures 1C](#page-2-0) and 1D). We also assessed a higher mRNA expression of *SMARCA5* in AML patients than that of normal controls in GEO: GSE13159[36](#page-17-9)[,37](#page-17-10) dataset [\(Figure S1B](#page-16-12)). Here, we illustrate that SMARCA5 is highly expressed in AML, while SMARCA5 is not uniformly overexpressed in CD34<sup>+</sup> AML cells compared with normal CD34<sup>+</sup> cells in all datasets.[

## 讨论与结论

Leukemia is a highly heterogeneous and devastating hematopoietic malignancy characterized by uncontrolled proliferation of LSCs and impaired differentiation of normal hematopoietic cells. Despite substantial efforts to treat leukemia, leukemia patients still suffer from treatment failure and relapse due to residual LSCs. Therefore, there is an urgent need to investigate the underlying mechanisms of leukemia to uncover effective targets and strategies for leukemia treatment.[6](#page-16-14)[,66](#page-18-13),[67](#page-18-14)

Normal developmental and pathological processes are closely related to the regulation of chromatin accessibility.[68](#page-18-15),[69](#page-18-16) Multiple studies have uncovered an abnormal chromatin accessibility spectrum in leukemia compared with normal hematopoiesis.[14](#page-16-5)[,15](#page-16-6) However, it is still elusive how abnormal chromatin accessibility occurs in leukemia patients. Thus, we performed a systematic analysis of epigenetic regulators through CRISPR screening. We find that SMARCA5 is the dominant chromatin remodeler responsible for abnormal chromatin accessibility in leukemia
