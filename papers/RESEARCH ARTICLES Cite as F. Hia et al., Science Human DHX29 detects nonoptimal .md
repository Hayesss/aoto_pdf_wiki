# RESEARCH ARTICLES Cite as: F. Hia et al., Science Human DHX29 detects nonoptimal codon usage to regulate

> 原文: [[research-articles-cite-as-f-hia-et-al-science-human-dhx29-detects-nonoptimal-cod]]

## 摘要

Synonymous codon usage controls global gene expression in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic species. Nonoptimal codons are known to induce mRNA decay; however, the underlying molecular mechanism remains poorly understood in human cells. Through genome-wide CRISPR screening, we identified the RNA-binding protein DHX29 as a critical regulator of codondependent gene expression. Cryogenic electron microscopy and selective ribosome profiling demonstrated that DHX29 directly interacts with the A-site entrance of the translating 80S ribosome, the binding site for the eEF1A•GTP•aminoacyltRNA ternary complex, suggesting a role in monitoring aminoacyl-tRNA sampling. Proteomic analysis further revealed that DHX29 recruits the GIGYF2•4EHP complex to mediate global suppression of nonoptimal mRNAs. These findings establish a mechanistic link between synonymous codon usage and the regulation of gene expression.

## 讨论与结论

In the current study, we discovered that the RNA-binding protein DHX29 plays a pivotal role in transcriptome-wide repression of nonoptimal mRNA expression in human cells. Our findings demonstrated that DHX29 directly interacts with the A-site entrance of the 80S ribosome, where its binding is mutually exclusive with that of the eEF1A•GTP•aminoacyl-tRNA complex. Moreover, we revealed that DHX29 more preferentially interacts with ribosomes decoding nonoptimal AU3 codons. Upon DHX29 binding to the A-site entrance, the GIGYF2•4EHP complex is recruited, likely initiating the decay of nonoptimal mRNAs and thereby influencing synonymous codon usage-dependent gene expression patterns (Fig. 5E).

Our selective Thor-Ribo-seq analysis revealed that DHX29 preferentially targets AU3 and AU-rich codons (Fig. 4), raising important questions about the basis of this specificity. Considering that DHX29 does not directly interact with mRNA codon sequences in the A site but instead sterically interferes with the eEF1A•GTP•aminoacyl-tRNA ternary complex (Fig. 2I), we speculate that A-site vacancy is the principal determinant of DHX29's codon preference (Fig. 5E). Clarifying this model will require precise measurement of the specific translation sub-step(s) at which DHX29 engages ribosomes in a codon-specific manner. However, no *in cellulo* approaches currently allow such resolution, and future studies will be required to define the mechanistic basis of DHX29's codon-specific activity.

Multiple proteins engage with the ribosome to monitor translation status and to induce a variety of quality control pathways (*1*, *4*, *5*). Our findings indicate that DHX29 plays a key role in sensing the A-site vacancy, a step proposed to precede CCR4-NOT recruitment to the E-site (*29*–*31*).
