## 主要发现

#### **YAP/TAZ promote SG disassembly in a Hippo-independent manner**

Aberrant SG behaviour is linked to the pathogenesis of AL[S10,](#page-11-1)[12](#page-11-3). To explore the involvement of Hippo-pathway components in SG dynamics, we initially examined their presence in SGs. This involved immunostaining with antibodies targeting either native proteins or epitope tags introduced via CRISPR-based knock-in (KI) techniques (Extended Data Fig. 1a–d). We observed that several components—that is, SAV1, LATS1, MOB1A, YAP and TEAD1—were noticeably present in SGs, whereas MST1 was not (Fig. [1a](#page-1-0) and Extended Data Fig. 1e–i). This led us to further investigate the role of the Hippo pathway in SG dynamics. We employed either small-interfering-RNA (siRNA)-mediated knockdown or CRISPR-based manipulation of Hippo proteins to observe the behaviour of SGs induced by NaAsO2 (Extended Data Fig. 2a–d). Neither deletion of *MST1* and *STK3* (gene encoding MST2; Extended Data Fig. 2e,f) or *LATS1* and -*2* (Extended Data Fig. 2g,h) nor *TEAD1*, -*3* and -*4* silencing (Extended Data Fig. 2i,j) affected the formation or disintegration of SGs. Similarly, pharmacological inhibition of MST1 and -2 using XMU-MP-1 (Extended Data Fig. 2k) did not markedly alter SG dynamics (Extended Data Fig. 2l,m). Intriguingly, genetic interference of *YAP1* (gene encoding YAP) and *WWTR1* (gene encoding TAZ) led to a noticeable delay in SG dissociation, whereas their formation remained mostly unchanged (Fig. [1b,c](#page-1-0)). These findings imply that YAP/ TAZ, as opposed to other Hippo-pathway components, may play a role in aiding SG disassembly, probably through mechanisms that are not directly related to the Hippo pathway.

## 讨论与结论

In this study, we revealed a role of YAP in controlling biomolecular condensates, including SGs and TDP-43 condensates. We found that YAP's interaction not only promotes the homotypic multimerization and phase separation of TDP-43 but also helps maintain TDP-43 fluidity, thereby preventing the solidification of cytoplasmic TDP-43 and SGs (Extended Data Fig. 9). Although direct YAP–TDP-43 molecular interactions are accountable for this function of YAP, YAP may also impact the binding affinity of TDP-43 to RNA that is involved in the dynamics of RNA-enriched TDP-43 condensates and SGs. Contrary to previous findings that showed YAP being passively retained in the cytoplasm and targeted for degradation[39](#page-11-24),[40,](#page-11-25) our study demonstrates YAP's active participation in altering the dynamics of biomolecular condensates. It is important to note that the presence of YAP in SGs was not reported in earlier studies identifying SG proteins through proteomic method[s27](#page-11-14)[–30](#page-11-15). This could be attributed to the relatively low abundance of YAP in SGs, making it undetectable by standard mass spectrometry methods. Under normal growth conditions, both YAP and TDP-43 are primarily located in the nucleus and their interactions are limited. However, under oxidative stress both YAP and TDP-43 relocate to cytoplasmic condensates. The upstream signalling mechanisms governing this process remain to be deciphered.

YAP/TAZ have highly similar sequences and structures[39](#page-11-24)[,40](#page-11-25). Until now most evidence supports their overlapping roles in the Hippo pathway[39,](#page-11-24)[40.](#page-11-25) However, our findings reveal that YAP/TAZ have distinct, non-redundant functions in regulating the dynamics of ALS-related biomolecular condensates.
