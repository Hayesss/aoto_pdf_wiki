## 主要发现

#### IRE1a RNase activity is inversely correlated with CD8+ T cell infiltration in response to taxane in patients with TNBC

Analysis of the gene expression data from the PROMIX trial showed that IRE1a target genes, including *XBP1s*, *EDEM3*, *SERP1*, *TSPAN13*, *SEC23B*, and *DNAJC10*, were among the genes that were negatively correlated with the cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) gene signature (*CD8*, *GZMB*, *PRF1*, etc.) and conventional type 1 dendritic cell (cDC1) genes (*BATF3* and *IRF8*) in HER2- breast cancer patients receiving neoadjuvant docetaxel/epirubicin treatment (Figures S1B and S1C; Table S1). Splicing of *XBP1* depends on and serves as a sensitive measure of IRE1a RNase activity.33–35 Using a selective antibody against XBP1s protein (Figures S1D and S1E), we analyzed paired preand post-treatment tumor tissues from TNBC patients receiving nab-paclitaxel neoadjuvant treatment. Nab-paclitaxel treatment induced CD8<sup>+</sup> T cell and TIL infiltration in 16 of 29 patients \(Figures 1A–1C) and increased the expression of antigen processing and presentation genes in these patients (Figure S1F). Patients displaying enhanced CD8<sup>+</sup> T cell and TIL infiltration in response to nab-paclitaxel treatment exhibited similar total *XBP1* but lower XBP1s levels than nonresponding patients \(Figures 1A, 1D, and S1G). Analysis of another 17 paired preand post-treatment tumor tissues from a docetaxel neoadjuvant clinical trial in TNBC patients independently confirmed the lower XBP1s levels in patients with enhanced CD8+ T cell and TIL infiltration after docetaxel treatment (Figures S1H–S1J). Survival analysis showed that TNBC patients with lower IRE1a RNase activity responded better to paclitaxel with longer relapse-free survival (Figure S1K). Collectively, these data demonstrate the reverse association of IRE1a RNase activity with taxane-induced TIL and CD8<sup>+</sup> T cell infiltration in patients with TNBC.

## 讨论与结论

Accumulating evidence suggests that standard-of-care chemotherapy can induce both noninflammatory apoptosis and inflammatory cell death that alerts the immune system depending on the agent, dose, and schedule. However, the mechanisms that dictate the form of chemotherapy-induced cancer cell death remain elusive. In this study, we identify IRE1a RNase as a checkpoint that restricts taxane-induced pore-forming pyroptosis in immunologically cold *TP53*-mutant TNBC. Taxanes are typically considered weak inducers of immunostimulatory cell death.,89 We found that IRE1a RNase activation silences taxane-induced danger signals that would otherwise activate the NLRP3 inflammasome and induce highly immunogenic pyroptosis. Reversing this checkpoint potently enhanced the immunogenicity of taxane, allowing taxane to induce extensive dsRNA accumulation and activate NLRP3-GSDMD pyroptotic signaling cascade, which, in turn, recruits and activates APC and initiates the antigen-specific T cell response.

dsRNA accumulates during viral infections.76 They also present in uninfected cells and serve as danger signal for pathologic cellular states in response to various pathological stresses.,90,91 Recent study shows that taxane induces dsRNAs in certain TNBC tumors, and taxane-resistant TNBCs silence dsRNA partially through epigenetic mechanisms.91 Mechanisms such as the epigenetic silencing of transposable elements and the resolution of dsRNA structures via ADAR1 have evolved to prevent the false activation of the immune system and autoimmunity. Our data suggest that the ER-localized ancient RNase IRE1a serves as another RNA regulatory mechanism for preventing dsRNA accumulation in response to taxane chemotherapy. This function of IRE1a is independent of its classical target XBP1 but is mediated, at least partially, through the RIDD activity, which degrades taxane-induced ER-localized RNAs containing both an IRE1a-recognition motif and repetitive elements in the 3 UTR.
