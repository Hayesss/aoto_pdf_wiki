## 背景与目的

Nucleophosmin 1 (NPM1), also known as B23, NO38 or numatrin, together with the isoforms NPM2 and NPM3, belong to the nucleophosmin/nucleoplasmin family of nuclear chaperones that are represented throughout the animal kingdom [[1](#page-10-0)]. Nuclear chaperones ensure proper nucleosomes assembly and also guarantee appropriate higher-order chromatin compaction. Another peculiar feature of NPM1 is the capability to shuttle between the nuclear and cytoplasmic compartments [[2](#page-10-0)], interacting with a variety of proteins and nucleic acids. Thanks to these chaperone and shuttling properties, NPM1 is involved in multiple cellular functions, including formation of the nucleolus through liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS), chromatin remodeling, regulation of nucleolar shape and ribosome biogenesis and transport, control of DNA repair and centrosome duplication as well as response to nucleolar stress [[3](#page-10-0)] (Supplementary Fig. 1). The structure of NPM1 protein have been previously reviewed extensively and is summarized in Supplementary Fig. 1. In this review, we focus on the functions of the native NPM1 protein and how its mutant contributes to promote and maintain acute myeloid leukemia (AML).

## 讨论与结论

Although multiple functions of the NPM1 protein have been described, others still remain to be discovered. Because the knowledge of functions of NPM1 is still limited, it is likely that what we currently know about the NPM1-driven mechanisms of leukemogenesis is only a partial view of the whole scenario. Endogenous tagging of wild-type and mutated NPM1 combined with mass spectrometry may assist in unraveling the interactome of these proteins in the cytoplasm, with the aim to identify novel cellular pathway that may be targeted with new drugs.
