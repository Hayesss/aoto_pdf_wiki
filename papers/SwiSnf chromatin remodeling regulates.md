## 背景与目的

Gene regulation underlies proper development, homeostasis, and cellular stress response. According to classical models, transcript levels directly correlate with protein synthesis. However, the high prevalence of non-coding transcription and variation in translation efficiency among transcript isoforms have unveiled deeper complexity in the RNA-to-protein relationship.[1–6](#page-15-0)

Recently, an unconventional form of gene repression was discovered where messenger RNA (mRNA) and protein levels are inversely correlated. For these genes, transcription from a distal promoter produces a 5<sup>0</sup> extended mRNA containing the downstream gene's entire coding sequence (CDS).[7](#page-15-1)[,8](#page-16-0) Upstream open reading frames (uORFs) in the extended mRNA's 5<sup>0</sup> leader restrict CDS translation. Accordingly, this distal mRNA isoform is termed the long undecoded transcript isoform (LUTI). LUTI transcription also interferes with transcription from the CDS-proximal promoter that controls expression of the coding mRNA isoform.[9](#page-16-1)[,10](#page-16-2) This combined transcriptional and translational interference reduces protein synthesis for the affected gene. Thus, LUTIs explain cases where mRNA and protein levels are poorly correlated.[8](#page-16-0)

LUTI-based gene repression is pervasive. In yeast, hundreds of genes have an associated LUTI that is expressed at distinct meiotic stages to temporally restrict protein synthesis.[8](#page-16-0) Additionally, cellular stress stimulates activation of LUTIs by the unfolded protein response (UPR) transcription factor (TF) Hac1 and during zinc starvation by the zinc-responsive TF Zap1.[11](#page-16-3)[,12](#page-16-4) Case studies have revealed that diverse gene targets are subject to LUTI-based repression, including the genes encoding the kinetochore subunit Ndc80,[7](#page-15-1)[,9](#page-16-1) the superoxide dismutase enzyme Sod1,[13](#page-16-5) the TF Swi4,[14](#page-16-6) and the purine hydrolase

#### A genetic approach to identify mutants defective in LUTI-based gene repression

To identify factors necessary for LUTI-based repression, we undertook a genetic approach. We generated two LUTI reporter alleles by fusing the 5<sup>0</sup> leader sequence of a well-characterized LUTI, *NDC80LUTI*, [7](#page-15-1)[,9](#page-16-1) to the *HIS3* and *ADE2* CDSs [\(Figure 1](#page-2-0)A). Cells with LUTI-based regulation intact are unable to grow without supplemented histidine when *HIS3LUTI* is expressed. Therefore, we first selected spontaneous mutants with disrupted LUTI-based repression that were able to grow during *HIS3LUTI* induction on media lacking histidine ([Figure 1C](#page-2-0); see [STAR](#page-19-0) [Methods](#page-19-0) for details). These were deemed ''LUTI escape mutants'' based on their inability to repress His3 expression. We next performed secondary screening on each mutant using the independent *ADE2LUTI* reporter phenotype: cells with LUTI regulation intact appear red, whereas cells with disrupted LUTIbased repression appear light pink or cream [\(Figure 1D](#page-2-0)).

To identify the causative mutations behind the LUTI escape phenotypes, we sequenced the genomes of cells exhibiting both *HIS3LUTI* and *ADE2LUTI* escape phenotypes. We identified and validated eleven mutations conferring LUTI escape phenotypes (see [STAR Methods](#page-19-0) for details). Strikingly, all LUTI escape mutations fell within genes encoding subunits of the Swi/Snf chromatin remodeling complex ([Figures 1](#page-2-0)B, [1F](#page-2-0), and [S1](#page-15-2)A). Our selection-based strategy uncovered mutations in six of the eight subunits that are specific to the Swi/Snf complex and are not members of other chromatin remodeling complexes,[24–26](#page-16-10) with five of the identified mutations falling within *SNF2*, which encodes the catalytic subunit [\(Figure 1](#page-2-0)B).

## 讨论与结论

Mutations in Swi/Snf subunits are found in -20% of all human tumors, making the complex one of the most commonly affected in cancer.[48](#page-17-7) A few studies have provided evidence in support of Swi/Snf acting in gene repression,[34](#page-16-27)[,49–53](#page-17-8) however, it has remained unclear whether Swi/Snf-dependent transcriptional repression occurs directly or indirectly.

In this study, we have demonstrated a direct role of the Swi/ Snf complex in transcriptional repression. This occurs by cotranscriptional nucleosome remodeling by Swi/Snf downstream of distal TSSs expressing non-canonical transcripts including LUTIs. Consequently, the NDR of the CDS-proximal promoter, which resides downstream of the distal TSS becomes nucleosome occupied, thereby resulting in the repression of the protein-coding transcript isoform ([Figure 7](#page-13-0), top). LUTI escape mutants *swi3-E815X* and *snf2-W935R* have minimal effects on Swi/Snf's ability to facilitate transcription initiation at its target loci. Instead, these mutations specifically disrupt nucleosome remodeling at positions downstream of the active TSS, resulting in the repression of the TSSPROX for Swi/Snf's TSSDIST targets ([Figure 7,](#page-13-0) middle). With more severe loss of Swi/Snf function, as in null or *snf2-Q928K* mutants, remodeling at the active TSS is reduced, leading to impaired transcription initiation. In these cases, transcriptional interference at the TSSPROX for Swi/Snf's TSSDIST targets is reduced indirectly, as a result of lower TSSDIST transcription ([Figure 7,](#page-13-0) bottom).
