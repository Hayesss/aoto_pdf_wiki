## 背景与目的

Colorectal cancer (CRC) exhibits B cell infiltration and/or tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS) formation, and the functional properties of tumor-infiltrating B cells and their effects on immunity are still controversial (Horii and Matsushita, 2021). Due to their antigen presentation and antibody production function, B cells have antitumor immunity, showing a positive association with improved cancer outcomes, particularly when they are found in association with TLS (Cabrita et al., 2020; Helmink et al., 2020; Petitprez et al., 2020). Multiple murine tumor models point to the existence of immune suppressive or regulatory B (Breg) cell subpopulations that can migrate into tumor beds and acquire a regulatory phenotype, such as high expression of inhibitory molecules including programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1), interleukin-10 (IL-10), and transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) (Liu et al., 2018; Shalapour et al., 2017), which then leads to attenuation of the local antitumor immune response. Further dissection of heterogeneous phenotypes of B cells within the tumor environment (TME) and the mechanisms for obtaining regulatory function may provide important avenues for modulation of the immune response and meaningful therapeutic intervention in CRC.

Nutrients drive cell metabolism and determine cell fate (Leone and Powell, 2020); however, the TME is nutrient barren, especially for glucose competition and consumption. Low glucose limits the differentiation and function of cytotoxic immune cells and regulatory T (Treg) cells in the TME. These cells use lipids, amino acids, and even tumor metabolites, such as lactic acid, to gain energy and maintain their survival and function (Bleve et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2019; Watson et al., 2021). Amino acids modulate the proliferative drive of tumor cells and have direct roles as protein synthesis substrates.
