## 背景与目的

Messenger RNA (mRNA) degradation and mRNA translation represent two fundamental steps in the regulation of gene expression. Stability of the mRNA affects mRNA levels ([Herzog et al., 2017](#page-20-0)) which in turn, impact protein production ([Ingolia, 2016](#page-20-0)). Alterations in mRNA degradation leads to developmental defects ([Giraldez et al., 2006](#page-20-0)) and human disease ([Goodarzi et al., 2016](#page-20-0)). Likewise, aberrant mRNA translation has been implicated in protein misfolding and neurodegenerative disease ([Kapur et al., 2017](#page-20-0); [Pechmann and Frydman, 2013](#page-20-0)), viral infection ([Walsh et al., 2013](#page-21-0)) and developmental defects ([Gonskikh and Polacek, 2017](#page-20-0); [Kong and Lasko, 2012](#page-20-0)). Recent studies have shown that translation impacts mRNA stability in a codon-dependent manner in yeast ([Harigaya and](#page-20-0) [Parker, 2016](#page-20-0); [Presnyak et al., 2015](#page-20-0); [Radhakrishnan et al., 2016](#page-21-0)), E. coli ([Boe¨l et al., 2016](#page-19-0)), zebrafish ([Bazzini et al., 2016](#page-19-0); [Mishima and Tomari, 2016](#page-20-0)), Xenopus ([Bazzini et al., 2016](#page-19-0)), Trypanosoma brucei ([de Freitas Nascimento et al., 2018](#page-19-0); [Jeacock et al., 2018](#page-20-0)) and Drosophila melanogaster ([Burow et al., 2018](#page-19-0)). This mechanism, termed codon optimality, refers to the ability of a given codon to affect mRNA stability in a translation-dependent manner ([Presnyak et al., 2015](#page-20-0)). mRNAs enriched in 'optimal' codons tend to be more stable, display greater abundance, higher translation efficiency and longer poly(A)-tails. Conversely, mRNAs enriched in 'non-optimal' codons tend to be unstable, display lower homeostatic RNA levels, poor translation efficiency and shorter poly(A)-tails ([Bazzini et al., 2016](#page-19-0); [Mishima and Tomari, 2016](#page-20-0); [Radhakrishnan et al., 2016](#page-21-0); [Webster et al.,](#page-21-0) [2018


## 主要发现

### Human mRNA decay correlates with codon composition in human cell lines

To determine whether codon composition affects mRNA stability in human cells, we treated 293T, HeLa and RPE cells with Actinomycin D to block transcription ([Figure 1—figure supplement 1A](#page-4-0)) and measured decay of existing mRNAs by performing time-course mRNA-seq ([Figure 1A](#page-3-0)). We calculated the codon stability coefficient (CSC) as the Pearson correlation coefficient between mRNA stability and codon occurrence. Based on this approach, we and others have previously determined the properties of each of 61 codons in mRNA stability in yeast ([Presnyak et al., 2015](#page-20-0)), zebrafish, and Xenopus embryos ([Bazzini et al., 2016](#page-19-0)) ([Figure 1A](#page-3-0)). Codons displaying a positive correlation were referred to as 'optimal' codons because mRNAs enriched in those codons were more stable. Conversely, codons displaying a negative correlation were referred to as 'non-optimal' codons because mRNAs enriched in those codons were less stable ([Figure 1A, B](#page-3-0)). These codon regulatory properties (optimal, non-optimal) correlate well across the three analyzed human cell types and zebrafish ([Figure 1C](#page-3-0) and [Figure 1—figure supplement 1B](#page-4-0)), suggesting that the regulatory activity of some codons is shared between humans and zebrafish. The CSC scores do not present strong correlation with codon usage ([Figure 1—figure supplement 1C](#page-4-0)). These results also imply that codon composition has effects on mRNA stability in humans that are analogous to those found in other species.

## 讨论与结论

The main function of the ribosome is to translate the mRNA nucleotide sequences into the amino acid sequence. However, translation is also important for mRNA quality control, targeting defective mRNAs for degradation (*Shoemaker and Green, 2012*). Translation has also been shown to strongly affect mRNA stability of non-defective transcripts depending on the codon composition in model organisms (*Bazzini et al., 2016*; *Boël et al., 2016*; *Burow et al., 2018*; *de Freitas Nascimento* 

<span id="page-13-0"></span>[et al., 2018](#page-19-0); [Harigaya and Parker, 2016](#page-20-0); [Jeacock et al., 2018](#page-20-0); [Mishima and Tomari, 2016](#page-20-0); [Presnyak et al., 2015](#page-20-0); [Radhakrishnan et al., 2016](#page-21-0)). Here, we demonstrate that, in human cells (also suggested in [Hanson and Coller, 2018](#page-20-0)), translation also strongly affects mRNA stability in a codondependent manner, thus dictating mRNA and protein levels at homeostasis (Figure 6). These observations highlight the wealth of regulatory information residing within the coding sequence, the largest fraction of the human transcriptome. Further, this study provides insight on the regulatory role of core components, such as tRNAs and ribosomes (Figure 6). These have long been under appreciated in regulation despite their relatively high levels of expression, their abundant interactions with mRNAs, and evolutionary conservation. Future studies of this relatively unexplored mechanism of post-transcriptional regulation may be relevant to human diseases.
