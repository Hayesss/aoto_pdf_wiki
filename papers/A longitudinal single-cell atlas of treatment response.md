## 背景与目的

Pediatric AML (pAML) is a heterogeneous disease frequently driven by genomic rearrangements. Despite advances in patient stratification, patient prognosis has not improved, and overall survival (OS) rates remain at $\sim\!75\%.^{2,3}$ Frequent relapse results in event-free survival (EFS) rates of only $\sim\!50\%.^{2,4}$ In contrast to adult AML (aAML), there is an absence of effective targeted therapies for most pAML cases, since tumors are molecularly distinct from adults and lack targetable driver alterations, such as IDH. Common driving aberrations found in pAML include translocations involving MLL ( $\sim\!20\%$ ), RUNX1 ( $\sim\!15\%$ ) and CBFB ( $\sim\!10\%$ ), internal tandem duplications in FLT3 ( $\sim\!20\%$ ; FLT3-ITD), and single nucleotide variants (SNVs) within CEBPA and genes involved in MAPK signaling (e.g., NRAS and KIT). pAML patients are typically treated using chemotherapies such as daunorubicin and cytarabine. Additions to standard chemotherapy, such as sorafenib and midostaurin, have shown some effectiveness in patients harboring *FLT3* alterations. <sup>10,11</sup> The proteasome inhibitor bortezomib (BTZ), <sup>12,13</sup> which is effec-

tive in multiple myeloma, $^{14,15}$ myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) and low-risk aAML, $^{16,17}$ failed to increase survival in pAML patients as shown by recent phase II clinical trials such as AAML1031. $^{12}$ 

Development and evaluation of new therapeutic strategies have been hampered by an incomplete understanding of the molecular changes that accompany pAML relapse. While studies have investigated molecular differences between pAML at diagnosis and relapse, <sup>18–21</sup> the distinct subpopulations of cells that shape relapsed tumors remain understudied.

## 讨论与结论

By analyzing a cohort of longitudinally collected pAML tumors representing multiple subgroups, we have described how relapsed pAML tumors differ substantially from tumors at diagnosis. We observed a general increase in cells residing in a primitive differentiation state upon relapse regardless of whether the initial tumors were predominantly composed of more differentiated cells, and we found evidence that the abundance of primitive cells at diagnosis may predict outcome. A similar association has been described in aAML,,[72,](#page-18-28) however signatures that are used to predict outcome in aAML are currently difficult to transfer to the different cytomolecular risk groups within pAML. Based on the hierarchies observed in distinct pAML subgroups, we argue that consideration of stem-like populations may aid in improving risk stratification of pAML patients and that the effectiveness of such stratification may increase when considered between tumors with similar driver alterations.[7,](#page-16-6)

Our study suggests that current therapy can eradicate myeloidprimed pAML cells, including CMP-like cells, GMP-like cells and cells resembling early monocytes, but not multipotent HSPClike cells. HSPC-like pAML cells displayed increased activation of other lineage specification programs at relapse, such as lymphoid and erythroid. These may lead to apparent lineage switching, as observed in two patients analyzed here. While the mechanisms behind the more lymphoid phenotype at relapse remain unclear, this is likely influenced by the driver alteration. eOCR activity, determined by comparing chromatin accessibility between malignant and normal HSPCs, differed between clusters defined by different driver alterations, possibly reflecting distinct differentiation potential across subgroups.
