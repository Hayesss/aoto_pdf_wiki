## 背景与目的

All cells are exposed to compounds that can damage their nucleic acids. This can be due to endogenous metabolites, as well as exogenous agents, such as certain chemotherapeutics used for cancer therapy. 1-4 A host of damage repair mechanisms has been described for DNA, which help to protect cells against the toxic and mutagenic effects of these compounds. However, RNA is also damaged after exposure to these agents and should not be ignored; indeed, the vast majority (>90%) of the nucleic acid damage induced by these sources, including alkylating agents and cisplatin, occurs in RNA.5,6 Compared with the well-studied mechanisms of DNA repair, the functional outcomes of RNA damage, or how damaged RNAs are processed in cells, are largely unknown. Recently, the functional significance of damaged RNAs in the cytosol, particularly on mRNAs during translation, has begun to be elucidated. For example, alkylated mRNAs are known to induce ribosome collisions and activate the ribosome quality control pathway, which functions to dissociate the stalled translational machinery from the damaged transcript. 7,8 It remains unclear whether alkylated RNAs or other types of RNA damage can have direct toxicity, particularly in the nucleus. An increasing body of work in the last two decades has shown the important roles RNA plays in DNA repair processes, 9,10 but how damaged RNAs impact genome integrity is unexplored.

# YTHDC1 and the THOC regulate alkylation responses

We hypothesized that YTH-domain proteins may be involved in alkylation damage responses via methylated RNA recognition and performed an shRNA (short hairpin RNA) screen individually targeting all human YTH-domain proteins [\(Figure S1](#page-14-17)A). Previous studies have shown that the ASCC complex forms nuclear foci in response to alkylation damage.[12](#page-14-18) We recapitulated this result and confirmed that the alkylating agent methyl methanesulfonate (MMS) indeed induced DNA damage signaling, as observed by H2A.X phosphorylation [\(Figure S1B](#page-14-17)); therefore, we quantified hemagglutinin (HA)-ASCC2 foci formation as a readout of this damage response. After treating with MMS, we found that loss of YTHDC1 significantly increased ASCC2 foci formation, but little effect was seen with loss of other YTH-domain proteins [\(Figures](#page-3-0) [1](#page-3-0)A and 1B; [Figure S1](#page-14-17)C). We previously showed that ASCC foci form primarily in the G1 phase[,12](#page-14-18) yet depleting YTHDC1 did not affect cell cycle distribution [\(Figure S1](#page-14-17)D), ruling out cell cycle artifacts. Together, these results suggested that YTHDC1 may be involved in a parallel alkylation response pathway.

Since ASCC is important for cell survival after alkylation stress, we tested the epistatic relationship between YTHDC1 and ASCC loss. Depleting either YTHDC1 or ASCC2 sensitized cells to MMS, but depleting both showed an additive effect on cell viability ([Figure 1](#page-3-0)C; [Figures S1E](#page-14-17)–S1H). To better understand the mechanism of YTHDC1 in the damage response, we identified its interacting partners by immunoprecipitation of YTHDC1 followed by mass spectrometry (IP-MS) using a HeLa-S cell line expressing an endogenously FLAG-tagged YTHDC1, with or without MMSinduced damage [\(Figures S2A](#page-14-17)–S2C; [Table S1](#page-14-17)). ## 讨论与结论

In this study, our work has altogether revealed two major findings: First, YTHDC1 and THOC are important for alkylation responses in cells in a manner that depends on the presence of an aberrant RNA methylation mark induced by chemical agents. Second, in the absence of YTHDC1 or THOC, these aberrantly methylated RNAs induce R-loops, which are then processed into DNA DSBs in a manner that depends on the XPG nuclease (Figure 7). These data provide the first definitive evidence that damaged RNAs impact genome integrity in the context of alkylation stress and identify a set of interacting proteins that prevent their accrual.

We initially found that loss of YTHDC1, as well as components of the associated THOC, caused a significant increase in the formation of nuclear ASCC foci upon alkylation stress (Figure 1). We had previously shown that these foci represent the recruitment of ASCC to nuclear speckle bodies, and this recruitment is dependent on K63-linked ubiquitination by the E3 ligase RNF113A, which is activated when nascent RNA Pol II tran-

scripts are damaged.<sup>15</sup> Therefore, we reasoned that the loss of YTHDC1 or THOC components is resulting in a greater accumulation of damaged nascent RNAs, causing an increased activation of RNF113A activity, which we validated experimentally (Figures 1H and 1I). Indeed, YTHDC1 associates with nascent RNAs containing damaged ribonucleosides, in particular m<sup>1</sup>A, and this binding is critical for its function in suppressing ASCC foci formation (Figure 2). This phenotype is likely attributable to methylated RNA recognition not only because of the importance of an intact YTH domain but also because expression of the m<sup>1</sup>A-specific demethylase BsV-AlkB in the nucleus rescued the increased ASCC foci phenotype. This strongly suggested that the YTHDC1, along with THOC, function in a manner that impacts the RNF113A-ASCC pathway.
