## 背景与目的

Charcot-Marie-Tooth (CMT) diseases are the most commonly inherited disorders affecting the peripheral nervous system with a prevalence of at least 1:2,500[.1](#page-17-0)[,2](#page-17-1) Over the past two decades, there have been over 30 mutated genes identified as associated with CMT2, an axonal form of CMT characterized by degeneration of peripheral axons leading to deficits in distal motor function.[3](#page-17-2) According to various causal genes, CMT2 can be further classified into different subtypes, each associated with a specific mutated gene. Despite similar clinical presentation among different CMT2 subtypes, their causal proteins are highly varied in their cellular localizations and functions. For example, the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases are the largest gene family implicated in CMT2, among which *GARS* (encoding glycyl-tRNA synthetase [GlyRS]) was the first member identified. Its mutations cause a dominant axonal form of CMT (CMT2D).[4,](#page-17-3)[5](#page-17-4) This enzyme is responsible for catalyzing the ligation of glycine to its cognate tRNA in protein synthesis. Contrastingly, CMT2B is caused by mutations in *RAB7A*, encoding a membrane-localized small GTPase that regulates endo-lysosomal trafficking and autophagy.[6](#page-17-5) In addition, CMT2B2 is associated with mutations in *PNKP*, which encodes a nuclear enzyme with both kinase and phosphatase activities involved in the repair of DNA damage.[7](#page-17-6)

#### GlyRS translocates into SGs upon stress

Previous genetics studies have identified over twenty missense *GARS* mutations in CMT2D patients with varying degrees of disease association. Among them, L129P and G240R are the ones most strongly linked to the disease.[22,](#page-17-19)[25](#page-17-20) The missense mutation (P234KY) was also found to causes CMT2D-like phenotypes in mice.[26](#page-17-21) To examine the localization of WT and mutant GlyRS upon exposure to stressors, we added the oxidative stress inducer, NaAsO2 (sodium arsenite, AS), into HeLa cells expressing V5-tagged WT GlyRS (GlyRSWT-V5) or CMT2D-associated GlyRS mutants (GlyRSCMT2D-V5). SG formation was visualized using the immunostaining of endogenous G3BP1. Using immunostaining of the V5 tag, we found that both GlyRSWT-V5 and GlyRSCMT2D-V5 proteins (P234KY, L129P, or G240R) were evenly distributed in the cytoplasm under normal conditions [\(Figures 1A](#page-2-0)–1C). Upon exposure to AS, both GlyRSWT-V5 and GlyRSCMT2D-V5 proteins formed discrete cytoplasmic foci, which were shown to be highly co-localized with G3BP1 [\(Figures 1A](#page-2-0)–1C) and other SG markers (such as G3BP2 and TIA-1) ([Figures S1A](#page-30-0)–S1D, S1G, and S1H). However, they were not co-localized with DCP1A, a protein specifically localized in P-bodies [\(Figures S1](#page-30-0)E, S1F, and S1I). These data indicate that environmental stress can induce cytoplasmic GlyRSWT and GlyRSCMT2D proteins to translocate into SGs.

## 讨论与结论

The accumulation of protein aggregates is a hallmark of many age-related neurodegenerative diseases.[18](#page-17-17),[43](#page-18-13) These diseases are also referred to as proteinopathies. Emerging evidence is beginning to highlight the important role of SGs in their pathogenesis. Genetic mutations, aging, and other chronic stresses induce the formation of persistent pathological SGs that act as a crucible for initiating protein aggregation. This then drives the integrated neurodegenerative disease pathways.[18](#page-17-17) Distinct from these age-related proteinopathies, the onset of CMT is relatively early, usually in adolescence or early adulthood. No obvious accumulation of protein aggregates is observed in the patients or animal models for many CMT subtypes.[22–24](#page-17-19) Therefore, the potential role of SGs in CMT pathogenesis has been elusive. In this study, we found that GlyRSCMT2D had little effect on SG dynamics but significantly perturbed the G3BP-centric core SG network, leading to SG functional deficiency in stress response ([Figures 2](#page-4-0) and [3\)](#page-7-0). We speculate that this mechanism may also be implicated in the pathogenesis of proteinopathies, in which SG network abnormalities may belong to one of the earliest pathological changes, occurring prior to the onset of protein aggregate accumulation. The subsequent protein aggregation may contribute to promoting the rapid progression and spreading of such diseases, as previously reported.[43](#page-18-13) Therefore, the lack of protein aggregates in CMT may explain the relatively slow progression and spread of this disease compared with ALS and other more devastating proteinopathies.
