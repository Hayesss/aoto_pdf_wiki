# HIF-1a drives cuproptosis resistance in the hypoxic tumor microenvironment

To investigate the role of hypoxia in cuproptosis, we conducted spatial transcriptomic sequencing (ST-seq) in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) and papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). We identified normoxic and hypoxic regions using hallmark gene sets associated with hypoxia and calculated cuproptosis and hypoxia scores based on known gene sets regulating these processes., A negative correlation was observed between the hypoxia score and cuproptosis score, and the hypoxic TME region showed a lower cuproptosis score, suggesting potential resistance to cuproptosis \(Figures 1A and 1B). ST-seq of PTC samples, external ST-seq of colorectal cancer (CRC) samples, and another ccRCC sample also support the negative correlation between hypoxia and cuproptosis \(Figures S1A and S1B). Moreover, we also validated the association between hypoxia and cuproptosis at single cell level in PTC. After identifying malignant epithelial cells from PTC, we clustered normoxia/hypoxia cell clusters. Hypoxic cell clusters were highly overlapped with cuproptosis-resistant cell clusters (low cuproptosis score, Figure 1C). Similar to that in the ST-seq, the hypoxia score showed a negative correlation with the cuproptosis score in single-cell analysis of PTC \(Figure 1D).

Next, we investigated the response curves of cancer cells to ES plus CuCl2 (ES + Cu) treatment under either normoxia or hypoxia condition. As expected, hypoxia significantly alleviated cuproptosis induced by ES + Cu stimulation in all tested cancer cell lines (Figure 1E), consistent with previous work demonstrated by Tsvetkov et al.14 In addition, we found that hypoxia promoted staurosporine-induced apoptosis, indicating specifical roles of hypoxia in cuproptosis resistance \(Figures S2A and S2B). ## 讨论与结论

While the concept of cuproptosis is relatively new, the toxic effects of copper have long been recognized. Currently, cuproptosis holds promise as a potential therapeutic target for genetic disorders associated with copper homeostasis, such as Menke's diseases. Copper-chelating agents, including TTM and D-penicillamine, have proven effective in treating Wilson's disease. However, the resistance of cuproptosis in solid tumor cells within the hypoxic TME severely hindered its application in cancer treatment.

Lipoylation is a conserved lysine posttranslational modification limited to a select group of proteins involved in the TCA cycle, including DLAT. DLAT is an integral component of the PDH complex, responsible for the decarboxylation of pyruvate within the TCA cycle to generate acetyl-CoA. PDK plays a crucial role in controlling the flow of pyruvate into cells, thus acting as a gatekeeper for the TCA cycle. Prior research has established HIF-1a as a transcription factor that activates PDK. In our study, we extended this knowledge by demonstrating that HIF-1a activates PDK1 and PDK3 to suppress DLAT expression, conferring resistance to cuproptosis.

Our finding is supported by previous studies demonstrating that PDKs are recruited and bound to E2 (DLAT),, resulting in enhanced PDK activity. Each isoform of PDKs exhibits different binding affinities for E2 (PDK3 > PDK1 z PDK2 > PDK4)., Meanwhile, we also identified a phosphorylation site (S100) of DLAT phosphorylated by PDK1 and PDK3. This phosphorylation activates the ubiquitination of DLAT itself. These results collectively demonstrate that PDK1 and PDK3 phosphorylate DLAT to promote its ubiquitination-mediated degradation.

In addition to the lipoylation system, copper homeostasis also plays a vital role in regulating cuproptosis. Copper homeostasis is primarily dependent on three key copper transport proteins: SLC31A1, ATP7A, and ATP7B.
