## 背景与目的

In eukaryotes, pervasive transcription by RNA polymerase II (-RNAPII) generates a variety of non-coding RNAs. 1,2 Furthermore, widespread premature termination events produce a large number of truncated RNAs. 3,4 The majority of these non-coding RNAs and truncated RNAs are retained in the nucleus and undergo swift turnover. 5-8 On the other hand, while most mRNAs are efficiently exported to the cytoplasm, a portion of them is selectively subjected to nuclear RNA degradation. 9 Given that most RNAs produced by RNAPII share apparent structural similarities, such as the 5' cap and the 3' poly(A) tail, accurately sorting these RNAs into the export or degradation pathway presents a significant challenge for cells.

The exosome complex (exosome) plays a crucial role in degrading various types of RNAs. 5-7,9-12 To recognize its wide range of substrates and achieve full activity, the exosome requires the association with multiple cofactors. One pivotal cofactor in this context is the RNA helicase MTR4, which, on its own, facilitates exosome function in the nucleus by unwinding high-order structures of its substrate RNAs. 9,13,14 Importantly, MTR4 interacts with distinct proteins to form different complexes, enabling the exosome to target various types of substrate RNAs in the nucleus. 6,7,15-17 One of these complexes is the poly(A) exosome targeting (PAXT) connection, which is involved in the degradation of spliced and polyadenylated transcripts, such as small nucleolar RNA (snoRNA) host gene (SNHG) transcripts and prematurely terminated RNAs (ptRNAs). 5-7,18 PAXT

## 主要发现

#### ZFC3H1 is prevalently loaded onto the first exon/intron of pre-RNAs

As a core component of PAXT, ZFC3H1 is thought to bind at the 3 end of spliced and polyadenylated RNAs, where it recruits the exosome for the degradation,19 (Figure 1A). To gain a transcriptome-wide view of ZFC3H1 binding, we performed enhanced crosslinking and immunoprecipitation sequencing (eCLIPseq). However, none of the commercially available ZFC3H1 antibodies worked for CLIP. Consequently, we generated a FLAG-EGFP-ZFC3H1 knockin (FLAG-ZFC3H1 KI) HeLa cell line and carried out eCLIP-seq using a FLAG antibody (Figures 1B and S1A–S1C). ZFC3H1 eCLIP-seq was biologically repeated three times, showing a strong correlation in binding among replicates \(Figure S1D).

We identified 65,119 ZFC3H1 binding peaks, with 88.41% mapped to protein-coding genes and 10.08% mapped to long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) genes \(Figure S1E). Surprisingly, the majority (91.49%) of the peaks were located in introns \(Figure 1C), suggesting that ZFC3H1 predominantly binds to pre-RNAs rather than spliced forms. Further, the metagene profile revealed that ZFC3H1 is almost exclusively distributed to the 5 end of RNAs \(Figure 1D), contrary to the previous belief that it binds to the 3 end.7,

Globally, ZFC3H1 was enriched on the first exon/intron of pre-RNAs (Figure 1E). Inspection of individual genes showed that on the first intron, ZFC3H1 mainly binds near the 5 splicing sites (SSs) \(Figure 1F). Considering that ptRNAs are major PAXT degradation substrates,,24 that frequently terminate in the first intron, it is possible that the 5 preferential ZFC3H1 binding might solely reflect its loading on ptRNAs. However, when ZFC3H1 binding targets were separated into pt genes and nopt genes based on whether ptRNAs are produced from a gene (see STAR Methods for details), ZFC3H1 was indiscriminately distributed to the first exon/intron of pre-RNAs in both subsets of genes (Figures S1F and S1G).
