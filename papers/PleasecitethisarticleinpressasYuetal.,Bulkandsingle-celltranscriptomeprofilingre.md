## 讨论与结论

The causes of metabolic heterogeneity are multifaceted. The emergence of high-content datasets and new sequencing techniques and tools for the analysis of these datasets, together with metabolic phenotype experiments, make it possible to explore the metabolic complexity of cancer. To our knowledge, this study is the first to establish a novel metabolic classifier for several breast cancer cohorts based on three energy sources: glucose, glutamine, and fatty acids. Cluster 1 is characterized by an increasing dependence on glycolysis and the PPP. In contrast, cluster 2 tumors prefer to utilize FAO and glutaminolysis for survival. An assessment of the variations between these clusters revealed specific changes in genomics, transcriptomics, and metabolomics, which suggested that metabolic heterogeneity should be considered when developing personalized therapies. Importantly, through the use of single-cell transcriptomics, we confirmed that malignant cells profoundly contribute to the metabolic heterogeneity of breast cancer and that the microenvironment also plays a role in metabolic plasticity. We also found that the metabolic difference between immune and stromal cell subtypes can be detected at the single-cell level, and the results suggested that immunosuppressive cells (e.g., dysfunctional CD8<sup>+</sup> T cells) were metabolically active. These results will aid the understanding of the metabolic heterogeneity in breast cancers and will provide novel comprehension for targeted therapy.

It has become increasingly clear that cancer cells exhibit heterogeneous metabolic preferences and dependencies.[4](#page-14-3) Understanding the emergence and evolution of metabolic variability and flexibility is important because it influences how we think about exploiting metabolic reprogramming for the treatment of cancer.
