# AJRCCM Articles in Press. Published on 27-August-2019 as 10.1164/rccm.201905-1013OC Page 1 of 59 Specific Inhibition of the NLRP3 Inflammasome as an Anti-Inflammatory Strategy in Cystic Fibrosis

> 原文: [[ajrccm-articles-in-press-published-on-27-august-2019-as-101164rccm201905-1013oc]]

## 摘要

RATIONALE: Cystic fibrosis (CF) pulmonary disease is characterized by chronic infection with *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and sustained neutrophil-dominant inflammation. The lack of effective anti-inflammatory therapies for people with CF (PWCF) represents a significant challenge.

OBJECTIVES: To identify altered immunometabolism in the CF neutrophil, and investigate the feasibility of specific inhibition of the NLR family, pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome as a CF anti-inflammatory strategy *in vivo*.

METHODS: Key markers of increased aerobic glycolysis, known as a Warburg effect, including cytosolic pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2), phosphorylated PKM2, succinate, HIF-1α, lactate and the interleukin (IL)-1β precursor pro-IL-1β, as well as caspase-1 activity and processing of pro-IL-1β to IL-1β by the NLRP3 inflammasome, were measured in neutrophils from blood and airway secretions from healthy controls (n=12), PWCF (n=16) and PWCF post-double-lung transplant (n=6). The effects of specific inhibition of NLRP3 on airway inflammation and bacterial clearance in a murine CF model were subsequently assessed *in vivo*.

MAIN RESULTS: CF neutrophils display increased aerobic glycolysis in the systemic circulation. This effect is driven by low-level endotoxemia, unaffected by CFTR modulation, and resolves post-transplant. The increased pro-IL-1β produced is processed to its mature active form in the lipopolysaccharide-rich CF lung by the NLRP3 inflammasome via caspase-1. Specific NLRP3 inhibition *in vivo* with MCC950 inhibited IL-1β in the lungs of CF mice (P<0.0001), resulting in significantly reduced airway inflammation and improved *Pseudomonas* clearance (P<0.0001).

CONCLUSIONS: CF neutrophil immunometabolism is altered in response to inflammation.

NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition may have an anti-inflammatory and anti-infective role in CF.


## 背景与目的

Interleukin (IL)-1β is a master inflammatory cytokine involved in the pathogenesis of insulin resistance, atherosclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, gout and Alzheimer's disease (1-5). IL-1β is also strongly implicated in pulmonary disease, where it stimulates mucus hypersecretion and airway hyperresponsiveness (6, 7), triggers further pro-inflammatory cytokine release (8), and impairs clearance of key pathogens such as *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Aspergillus* (9-11). The list of immune cells capable of producing IL-1β includes monocytes, tissue macrophages, Tlymphocytes, dendritic cells and neutrophils (12, 13). Human IL-1β is synthesized as the precursor protein pro-IL-1β and requires cleavage to a mature, active form to exert its proinflammatory effect, this cleavage typically occurring via the class I cysteine protease caspase-1 (14, 15). Caspase-1 itself requires activation, initiated by inflammasomes in response to activation of pattern recognition receptors such as toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) by environmental, pathogenic or endogenous danger signals (16). The NLRP3 inflammasome is a composite of adapter and effector proteins highly expressed in myeloid cells, containing NLRP3, adapter protein apoptosis-related speck-like protein (ASC), and caspase-1 (16). Previous studies have identified NLRP3 as a key sensor for phagocytosed material, insoluble protein aggregates and metabolic products (1, 2, 4, 17-19) and the driver of IL-1β.

It has become increasingly clear that increased transcription of pro-IL-1β in response to infection or tissue injury is fueled by changes in cellular metabolism. In healthy cells, aerobic metabolism mostly occurs via an intact tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and oxidative phosphorylation to generate adenosine triphosphate (ATP), while glycolysis is responsible for anaerobic metabolism.


## 主要发现

**IL-1β levels are increased in CF airway secretions and correlate with markers of clinical outcome.**

IL-1β was increased in BAL from *F508del*/*F508del* PWCF (CF, n=12) compared to healthy controls (HC, n=12; 316.2+/-30.87pg/mL vs 39.94+/-2.1pg/mL, P<0.0001, Fig. 1A). IL-1β levels in BAL from *F508del*/*F508del* PWCF post-double lung transplant (Tx, n=6; 45.48+/- 8.434pg/mL) matched those from HC. BAL from patients with primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD), a genetic condition that results in the development of severe bronchiectaisis (119+/-45.63 pg/mL) also displayed increased IL-1β levels compared to HC and Tx (P=0.01), with levels less than for PWCF (P=0.009). CF BAL was neutrophil-predominant with neutrophils accounting for 71% of total cell count on average, consistent with previously published studies (42, 43). BAL neutrophil percentage correlated strongly with IL-1β levels (R=0.92, P<0.0001, Fig. 1B), suggesting that the CF airway IL-1β burden was mainly neutrophil-derived. IL-1β in CF BAL correlated with FEV1 (R=0.83, P<0.0001, Fig. 1C). This correlation was maintained when applied to CF sputum (n=40; R=0.9, P<0.0001, Fig. 1D). IL-1β levels in CF BAL (Fig. 1E) and sputum (Fig. 1F) also correlated with clinical outcome as assessed by the CF-ABLE score (R2=0.85, R2=0.93, respectively, both P<0.0001). Collectively, these results demonstrate that IL-1β is a clinically relevant readout of neutrophil-predominant airway inflammation in CF.

## 讨论与结论

In this study, we show for the first time that the CF neutrophil displays Warburg metabolism in the systemic circulation. This shift relies on an intact PKM2/succinate/HIF-1α axis and results in increased production of IL-1β, it's processing at the lung driven by the NLRP3 inflammasome via caspase-1. This inflammatory effect is abrogated both by inhibition of glycolysis and specific inhibition of NLRP3 by small molecules *in vivo*, identifying the inflammasome as a therapeutic target in CF.

PKM2 and phosphorylated PKM, previously identified as pivotal components of Warburg metabolism, are increased in the cytosol of circulating CF neutrophils. This phenomenon is amenable to inhibition by blocking glycolysis with 2DG. PKM2 expression is not influenced by CFTR modulation, and both PKM2 and phosphorylated PKM2 expression in circulating CF

neutrophils return to HC levels following lung transplantation. In essence, this replacement of infected bronchiectatic CF lungs with healthy ones represents removal of a focus of infection and inflammation, akin to incision and drainage of an abscess. The potential influence of immunosuppression on this result should be acknowledged, in particular the risk of alterations in glucose metabolism with steroid and calcineurin inhibitor treatment, but the standard posttransplant immunosuppressant regime would also, if anything, be considered anti-inflammatory. Taken together, these data suggest that the effects observed are inflammatory rather than intrinsic, and occur via the glycolytic pathway.

The remaining pillars of Warburg effect are similarly increased in the CF neutrophil. Levels of the TCA intermediate and inflammatory danger signal succinate are increased in CF neutrophil cytosols and return to HC levels following transplantation. Succinate acts to stabilise the transcription factor HIF-1α, which is readily detectable in CF neutrophil cytosols.
