## 背景与目的

Various types of myeloid cells play an important role in innate immunity; they are mostly derived from the hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs).1, Knowledge of myeloid hematopoietic progenitors is crucial for understanding how lineage diversity originates and how the abnormality of myelopoiesis may induce hematological or immunological dysfunction in disease.

In the canonical hematopoietic model, myeloid lineages are classified as granulocytic and monocytic, mainly distinguished by their morphological, histological, and immunophenotypic features.3 Recent advances in single-cell technology have promoted investigations of myeloid development on a molecular scale.4–9 Based on this, questions have been raised regarding the developmental pathways of myeloid lineages, which might challenge the conventional theory., However, due to limitations in the number of cells examined in the previous studies and the absence of benchmarking comparisons across species and developmental stages, further comprehensive investigations are required to elucidate the ontogeny of myeloid lineage diversity.

Granulocyte-monocyte progenitors (GMPs) are considered a hub in myelopoiesis; they harbor potential toward various types of myeloid lineages in the mouse, including monocytes/macrophages, neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, and mast cells,10 \(Figure 1A). Previous studies have shown that GMPs are a heterogeneous group of progenitors rather than an entire multipotent population.6, Therefore, they serve as a typical population to study the progenitors of various myeloid lineages. Common myeloid progenitors (CMPs) and lymphoid-primed multipotential progenitors (LMPPs) represent the intermediate populations along the developmental pathways from multipotent progenitors (MPPs) to GMPs.1,,12 Collectively, these progenitor populations constitute a continuum that serves as the origin of myeloid lineage diversity (Figure 1A).

# Single-cell analysis of myeloid progenitors reveals a conserved lineage specification program during myelopoiesis

To understand the process of myeloid lineage development, we first performed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) on GMPs (Lin<sup>-</sup>c-Kit<sup>+</sup>Sca-1<sup>-</sup>CD34<sup>+</sup>CD16/32<sup>hi</sup>) isolated from three adult mouse bone marrow samples (Figures 1A and S1A). After quality control, we obtained 26,414 high-quality cells with an average profile of 3,248 genes per cell and a total of 20,983 genes detected across all cells. Subsequently, we integrated the data from each sample and annotated them with canonical lineage markers (Figures 1B, S1B, and S1C). We identified 7 lineage-committed subtypes within the GMP population, encompassing progenitors for monocytes (proMo), neutrophils (proNeu), eosinophils (proEo), basophils (proBa), mast cells (proMa), megakaryocytes and erythrocytes (proME), and dendritic cells (proDC). Cells displaying limited or no expression of

lineage-specific markers were annotated as uncommitted progenitors. This result demonstrated the remarkable heterogeneity within the GMP population, providing a compelling explanation for the diverse lineage potential exhibited by GMPs.

Interestingly, we found that committed GMPs predominantly formed two compartments on the uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) and that proBa, proMa, and proEo clustered within one compartment, while proNeu and proMo resided in the other (Figure 1B). Principal-component analysis (PCA) displayed a consistent bifurcation among myeloid-lineage progenitors (Figure 1C). Notably, this segregation is inconsistent with the canonical classification of myeloid lineages as monocytic and granulocytic categories. To validate the observations, we conducted a transcriptional correlation analysis among the myeloid progenitors.

## 讨论与结论

Myeloid cells include a variety of lineages, each wielding a substantial influence over the immune systems of vertebrates. In this

study, through a comprehension of cellular, molecular, developmental, and evolutionary views, we revisit the question of how the diversity of myeloid lineages forms. By single-cell transcriptomic and lineage tracing analyses, we identify a distinct lineage specification process separating myeloid cells into the NM lineage and EBM lineage. Notably, this specification phenomenon is universal during the individual development of humans, and it is extensive in the hematopoiesis of other vertebrates, including monkey, mouse, and zebrafish. Specifically, the program is characterized by the divergent expression of *Ikzf2* in EBMPs and NMPs, a pattern conserved across species. Through flow cytometry analysis, we validate the biased expression of IKZF2 in lineage-specified progenitors of EBM lineage, such as pro-BMPs, pre-BMPs, BMCPs, BaPs, and EoPs. Moreover, differentiation experiments using an *Ikzf2*-EGFP reporter mouse model demonstrate that *Ikzf2*-EGFP<sup>+</sup> and *Ikzf2*-EGFP subsets of myeloid progenitors, including CMPs and GMPs, represent different populations specified toward the EBM and NM lineages. Further downstream tracing of myeloid progenies reveals a distinct segregation of eosinophils, basophils, and mast cells from neutrophils, monocytes, and their derived macrophages, which is clearly distinguished by *Ikzf2*-EGFP expression. Together, based on the comprehensive transcriptome analyses and the experimental investigations facilitated with the marker IKZF2, our findings on myeloid progenitors and progenies raised questions regarding the conventional theory of myeloid lineage classification.
