## 背景与目的

Abnormal metabolism is a hallmark of cancer. Most cancer cells are addicted to aerobic glycolysis even in the presence of oxygen. This metabolic alteration, also known as ''Warburg effect,'' primes the microenvironment and brings about elevated glycolytic intermediates such as lactate to support cell proliferation., Evidence indicates that lactate is involved in regulating various biological processes such as immune cell differentiation, tumor immunosurveillance, fibrosis, ischemic injury, and regulation of gene expression via an epigenetic modification called lactylation.[6–8](#page-16-0) The latter showed that lactate can covalently modify proteins, but the mechanism by which intracellular lactate is detected and converted to lactylation remains unknown.

The tumor suppressor p53 mediates multiple functions; as a transcription factor, it safeguards cells against malignant transformation by activating numerous targets that control DNA repair, cell cycle progression, and survival under stresses. p53 activity is often suppressed during tumorigenesis, and more than half of the human cancers harbor p53 mutations that compromise p53 tumor-suppressor activity., p53 activity is also regulated by post-translational modifications, such as phosphorylation, acetylation, and ubiquitination. Understanding the mechanisms underlying p53 regulation may broaden the therapeutic strategies to boost p53 activity in patients with cancer.

In this study, we performed a CRISPR screen, which identified AARS1 as an intracellular lactate sensor that directly binds to lactate and catalyzes global lysine lactylation, including p53. Through the ATP-dependent formation of lactate-AMP, AARS1 transfers lactate to conjugate covalently to lysine residues and mediates the formation of a proteome-wide lysine lactylome. ## 主要发现

#### Tumor-derived lactate is a natural suppressor of p53 that promotes p53 lactylation

In evaluating the TCGA breast cancer dataset set, we found that among the patients harboring wild-type p53, those with gene signatures representing high serum lactate level showed a significantly lower score of p53 signaling pathway ([Figures 1A](#page-2-0) and S1A), suggesting that lactate in tumor might dampen p53 function. In the mouse mammary tumor virus (MMTV)-polyoma middle T antigen (PyMT) transgenic model of breast cancer, tumor lactate levels increased during tumor development and were positively correlated with tumor burden (Figure S1B), suggesting that intracellular lactate accumulation may facilitate tumor progression. To investigate the role of lactate *in vivo*, we intraperitoneally injected NOD scid gamma (NSG) mice with sodium lactate, the basic form of lactate, before and after inoculation with MMTV-PyMT tumor cells (Figure S1C). We found that sodium lactate injection led to earlier formation of palpable tumors (20 mm3 ) and significantly greater tumor volume [\(Fig](#page-2-0)[ure 1B](#page-2-0)); moreover, the p53 activity was significantly repressed in tumors from mice administered sodium lactate ([Figure 1C](#page-2-0)). Even in response to irradiation, the induction of p53 target genes and apoptotic cell death in the mouse thymus were reduced in the animals treated with sodium lactate (Figures S1D–S1F).

Lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA) converts pyruvate to lactate, and high LDHA expression correlated with poor survival in patients with breast cancer (Figure S1G). To consolidate the above findings, PyMT animals expressing the MMTV-driven *Cre*-recombinase were crossed with animals homozygous for an *Ldha* allele harboring *loxP* sites (*Ldha*fl/fl) (Figure S1H), enabling the constitutive deletion of monoallelic *Ldha* in the mammary epithelium ([Figure 1D](#page-2-0)).

## 讨论与结论

Tumor cells often reprogram their metabolism to increase lactate production. Here we found that tumor-derived lactate antagonized p53 via lactylation. A genome-wide CRISPR screen identified AARS1, an ancient enzyme known for its role in protein translation, as a lactate sensor and a lactyltransferase responsible for the global lysine lactylome. We identified two residues within the p53 DBD, K120 and K139, as bona fide targets of AARS1 and demonstrated that the lactylation blocks p53 LLPS, binding to DNA, and induction of its target genes, contributing to tumorigenesis (Figure 7P).

In general, two or more enzymes are required to catalyze protein acylations, as exemplified by the process of acetylation. In contrast, the lactylation uncovered in this study is mediated by a single enzyme, the evolutionarily conserved AARS1.

ARSs may fail to discriminate their cognate amino acid, leading to incorrect amino acid incorporation. For example, AARS1 can transfer not only alanine but also serine or glycine with profound functional consequences. <sup>22,24,55</sup> The lactate-binding capacity of AARS1 is explained by the similar molecular structure of alanine and lactate. The lactyltransferase activity of AARS1 suggests a pleiotropic nature of this conserved enzyme. While our study was submitted for re-review, a paper documented a similar role of AARS2, the mitochondrial homolog of AARS1, in the lactylation of mitochondrial proteins.
